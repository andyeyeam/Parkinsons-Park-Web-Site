section, numbered in sequence. To keep the feature fresh, **never reuse a fact from this

## Candidate facts for future issues (not yet used)
Reserve/refresh this list as you go so there's always a ready pick:

- Frank Parkinson founded F & A Parkinson in 1913 from a shed with just **£21** of postal savings; the firm became Crompton Parkinson, and his £1.5M legacy funded the **Parkinson Building at Leeds University**.
- Two 1950s **"Coronation Copses"** were planted in the shapes of the **outlines of Yeadon and Guiseley** to mark the Queen's Coronation and the Festival of Britain — and still stand.
- The park's sledging runs had names: **"Snow Drop"** (steep Great Brow) and the **"Bluebell Run"** (gentle Crooked Lands slopes).
- **Clapper Brow** takes its name from Middle English for "rabbit hole" — the site of a **medieval commercial rabbit warren**.
- Jonathan Peate planted oak trees around **1909** for King George V's coronation; several of **Peate's Oaks** still stand in the park.
- **Anglo-Saxon lynchets** (terraced field boundaries) pre-dating 1066 are still visible in the **Crooked Lands** field.
- The park's oldest trees are roughly **200-year-old sycamores** and **100-year-old oaks**.
